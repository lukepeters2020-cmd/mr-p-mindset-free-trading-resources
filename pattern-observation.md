# Observe a pattern without assuming its outcome

Chart shapes describe historical price arrangements. A label alone does not establish a profitable strategy or predict what happens next.

## Schematic resistance example

```text
Price
  |        peak         peak       ?
  |--------- resistance area -----------
  |      /     \      /     \
  |_____/       \____/       \___
  +------------------------------------> time
```

This is a drawing, not an observed chart or signal. Real levels are often areas rather than exact lines.

## Record before revealing later bars

1. Instrument, venue and currency.
2. Data source, timestamp and whether the series is generated or historical.
3. Candle interval and session.
4. The area you labelled and why.
5. What later observation would contradict the label.
6. Alternative explanations: sideways range, temporary excursion, gap or changing liquidity.

Reveal a fixed number of bars and write what happened. Keep the original annotation so you do not redraw a successful-looking pattern after seeing the future. Record failed observations as carefully as successful-looking ones.

## A more useful review question

Instead of “Did I find a perfect signal?”, ask “Did I define the observation before looking ahead, and can another learner reproduce it?” A future strategy test also needs costs, data quality, an adequate sample and out-of-sample checks.

Never treat a screenshot or an educational pattern name as personalized trading advice. Practice does not confer a professional qualification or permission to trade live.
